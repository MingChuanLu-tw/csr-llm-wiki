# Card C8 — What four other models say

Source page: [[rival-exclusion]] · [[csr-modelling-choices]] · [[sources]]

⚠️ All facts below come from **light-depth** sources: read and summarised, **no
symbolic verification, no independent review**. A number from here may not go on
a slide without being checked first ([[sources]]).

| # | Fact | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| C8.1 | Three independent models drive a rival to zero output: Kopel at $\gamma_{\max}$; Chen's Model CN at its optimum; Bárcena-Ruiz at $\beta\ge(3-\sqrt5)/2\approx0.3819$. | Kopel p. 495; Chen Lemma 1 p. 179; Bárcena-Ruiz Lemma 2 p. 375 |
| C8.2 | The **same corner** is treated as a technical bound (Kopel), a result (Chen: "a monopoly can lead to a higher CSR level"), and a deliberate strategy (Bárcena-Ruiz: shareholder B picks the CSR level that zeroes firm 1's profit). | as above |
| C8.3 | The five papers formalise "CSR" five different ways: demand-intercept investment (Kopel), objective weight + demand shifter (Chen), emissions cap (Hirose & Matsumura), consumer-surplus weight chosen by a shareholder (Bárcena-Ruiz), delegation parameter (Xing & Lee). | [[csr-modelling-choices]] |
| C8.4 | **A result in one category does not transfer to another.** Kopel's conclusion is about *appropriable demand shifts*; it does not imply an emissions cap fails to pay. | [[csr-modelling-choices]] |
| C8.5 | Hirose & Matsumura treat ECSR explicitly as "a commitment device to soften competition" — **the same engine as Kopel's, applied mutually rather than sequentially**, so the appropriability problem does not arise in the same way. ⚠️ unverified source. | Hirose & Matsumura abstract, p. 1 |
| C8.6 | Xing & Lee: $h^{NN}=0$ — **owners adopt no ECSR at all** unless they can cooperate on it. | Xing & Lee Prop. 1, p. 2688 |
| C8.7 | Convergent negative conclusion across four of five: unilateral CSR pays the firm that does it only when something stops the rival from getting the benefit — and in most specifications nothing does. | [[csr-modelling-choices]] |
| C8.8 | ⚠️ Symbol clash: $\theta$ = spillover rate (Kopel) but CSR level (Bárcena-Ruiz); $\beta$ = CSR level (Chen) but cross-ownership share (Bárcena-Ruiz). | [[glossary]] |
