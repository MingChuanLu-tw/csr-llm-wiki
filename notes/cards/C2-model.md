# Card C2 — Model setup

Source page: [[Kopel2021-CSR]] §4 · [[equations]] · [[glossary]]

| # | Fact | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| C2.1 | Two firms, horizontally differentiated products, constant unit cost $c$, reservation price $a$. | Kopel p. 493 |
| C2.2 | $\gamma \in [0,1)$ is substitutability; higher $\gamma$ = more intense competition. | Kopel p. 493 |
| C2.3 | Firm $i$ chooses CSR level $s_i$ at cost $s_i^2$. | Kopel p. 493 |
| C2.4 | $\theta_i$ is the share of the **rival's** CSR benefit captured by firm $i$ (read off the equation, not the prose). | Kopel pp. 493–494; A3 F3 |
| C2.5 | Kopel sets $\theta_1 = 0$, $\theta_2 = \theta$: the follower free-rides on the leader, not vice versa. **This is an assumption, not a result.** | Kopel p. 494 |
| C2.6 | Inverse demand (K-1): $p_1 = a + s_1 - x_1 - \gamma x_2$, $p_2 = a + (s_2+\theta s_1) - x_2 - \gamma x_1$. | Kopel eq. (1), p. 494 |
| C2.7 | Profits: $\pi_i = (p_i - c)x_i - s_i^2$. | Kopel p. 495 |
| C2.8 | CSR enters as a **vertical** demand shift; $\gamma$ is **horizontal** differentiation. | Kopel p. 494 |
| C2.9 | $\theta$ measures **appropriability** — "the firm's ability to capture or internalize the benefits of a CSR programme". | Burke & Logsdon (1996, p. 497), quoted Kopel fn. 6, p. 494 |
| C2.10 | Patagonia is "quite successful in differentiating its products and defending its advantage in a premium-segment of the market"; StarKist's dolphin-safe policy was imitated "almost immediately". | Kopel fn. 7, p. 494 |
| C2.11 | The $\theta_1\le\theta_2$ justification, verbatim: "it seems plausible to assume that $\theta_1 \le \theta_2$, because the CSR follower can free ride on the CSR efforts of the leader." ⚠️ A5-11 records a second wording ("sensible") still quoted on two wiki pages — unresolved. | Kopel p. 494 |
| C2.12 | $\gamma_{\max}$ justification, **whole clause**: it "guarantees that the equilibrium of the game is in the interior and all second-order conditions are satisfied". The paper does not say which condition binds. | Kopel p. 495 |
