# Card C10 — Discussion prompts

Source page: [[open-questions]]

| # | Question | Why it bites |
|---|---|---|
| C10.1 | Is $\theta_1=0$ doing too much work? | The paper's question is *who benefits from whom*; assuming the leader benefits by exactly zero is not neutral. A follower who "copies **and improves**" (p. 494) would create a reverse spillover the model excludes. **Testable with the existing sympy model.** |
| C10.2 | Would anyone *choose* to be the leader? | The conclusion is that leading usually loses. With endogenous timing both firms would wait — a war of attrition, which this model cannot represent. Kopel lists endogenous timing as an extension (p. 502). |
| C10.3 | Is a result about an unmeasurable parameter actionable? | The FMA needs $\theta<0.0015$ at $\gamma=0.3$ **[own calculation]**, and the paper concedes the answer "depends on factors which are quite hard to measure accurately" (p. 491). Is there an observable proxy? |
| C10.4 | Does the U-shape reconcile the conflicting literature? | If the true relation is U-shaped **and** role-dependent, a linear specification recovers whichever sign the sample spans. **[our reading]**, not Kopel's. |
| C10.5 | Is rival exclusion a prediction or an artefact? | Three models hit it, all with linear demand, no fixed costs, no entry. |
| C10.6 | How much depends on linear demand and quadratic cost? | The U-shape balances a markup and a demand effect; both are functional-form dependent. Kopel lists a demand-*slope* version as an extension (p. 502). |

**Do not raise** the $\delta^{**}\approx0.531$ / $\gamma\approx0.532$ numerical
coincidence ([[open-questions]] Q9) — it has not been derived.
