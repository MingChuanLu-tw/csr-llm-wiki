# Card C4 — Benchmark: no spillovers ($\theta = 0$)

Source page: [[Kopel2021-CSR]] §6 · [[competition-intensity]] · [[numbers]]

| # | Fact | Citation |
|---|---|---|
| C4.1 | Gap formula: $s_1^*-s_2^* = \frac{\gamma^2(2+\gamma)}{(1+\gamma)(3-\gamma^2)(6-\gamma(4+\gamma(5-\gamma-\gamma^2)))}s_1^* > 0$ for $0<\gamma\le\gamma_{\max}$. | Kopel p. 497 |
| C4.2 | **Lemma 1**: at $\theta=0$, $s_1^* > s_2^*$ **and** $\pi_1^* > \pi_2^*$. An FMA exists. | Kopel p. 499 (verbatim quote available) |
| C4.3 | The paper gives **no proof** — "follows from a simple comparison is therefore omitted" (*sic*). Confirmed numerically on 39 grid points. | Kopel p. 499; A4 §3 |
| C4.4 | $\partial s_1^*/\partial\gamma<0$ for $\gamma<0.531963$, $>0$ above — the leader's CSR is **U-shaped**. $s_2^*$ falls throughout. | Kopel p. 497; recomputed 0.5319633163 |
| C4.5 | Three effects: **markup** (negative in $\gamma$, both firms), **demand** (positive, both — stated for the no-spillover case), **strategic** (positive, **leader only**). | Kopel pp. 498–499, fns. 11–12 |
| C4.6 | The strategic effect makes the leader **over-invest**: fn. 14 gives $\frac{\partial\pi_1}{\partial s_2}\frac{\partial s_2}{\partial s_1}>0$. | Kopel p. 499 |
| C4.7 | The leader's profit advantage **grows** with competition: it "can not only be sustained, but made even larger". | Kopel p. 499 |
| C4.8 | ⚠️ At $\gamma=0$ the two curves coincide at $1/3$ — "the leader always invests more" holds only for $\gamma>0$. | A3 r2, from Fig. 1 |
