# Rival exclusion: when strategic CSR drives the other firm to zero

**Sources so far**: [[Kopel2021-CSR]] · [[Chen2021-OptimalCSR]] · [[BarcenaRuizSagasta2021-CrossOwnershipCSR]] (3 of 5)

> **[our reading].** This page is a cross-source pattern, not a claim any single
> paper makes. None of the three authors cites the others on this point.

## The pattern

Three independent models of strategic CSR — different competition modes,
different parameters, different questions — each contain a corner where **one
firm's output falls to zero and the duopoly collapses to a monopoly**, and in
each case the corner is reached by *increasing the strategic asymmetry* rather
than by any cost or demand shock.

| Source | Competition | Driving parameter | Who exits | At |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[Kopel2021-CSR]] (p. 495; A4) | Bertrand, differentiated | $\gamma$ (substitutability) | the **follower** | $\gamma_{\max} = 0.810736$, at $\theta = 0$ |
| [[Chen2021-OptimalCSR]] (Lemma 1, p. 179) | Cournot, homogeneous | which firms adopt CSR | the **non-adopter** (firm 2) | at the optimum of Model CN |
| [[BarcenaRuizSagasta2021-CrossOwnershipCSR]] (Lemma 2, p. 375) | Cournot, homogeneous | $\beta$ (cross-ownership) | the **controlled** firm 1 | $\beta \ge (3-\sqrt5)/2 \approx 0.3819$ |

## Why it is worth a page

**It changes how a boundary condition should be read.** In Kopel,
$\gamma < \gamma_{\max}$ is presented as a technical restriction "so that the
equilibrium is in the interior" (p. 495). Taken alone that reads as housekeeping.
Seen against the other two, it looks like the same economic event those papers
treat as a *result*: strategic CSR, pushed far enough, excludes the rival.

**It bounds the headline claim.** Kopel's statement that the leader's advantage
"can not only be sustained, but made even larger" as products become homogeneous
(p. 499) is evaluated right up against the point where the follower leaves the
market. The advantage grows partly because the rival is disappearing. See
[[competition-intensity]].

**It is a discussion opening.** A model whose interesting region ends where the
duopoly ends is telling you something about the model, and all three papers hit
it. Worth asking the room whether the exclusion is an artefact of linear demand
with no entry, or a real prediction about CSR-intensive industries.

## What the three do *not* share

- **Kopel**: exclusion is a *bound on the analysis* — the paper restricts $\gamma$ to stay inside it.
- **Chen**: exclusion is a *result* — Model CN's optimum is a monopoly, and the authors read it as "a monopoly can lead to a higher CSR level" (p. 179).
- **Bárcena-Ruiz**: exclusion is a *strategy* — shareholder $B$ "chooses the CSR level that makes the profits of firm 1 zero" deliberately (p. 375), and the authors note the government may then prefer monopoly to duopoly (p. 370).

So the same corner is housekeeping, a finding, and an instrument, depending on
who is looking. That difference is itself the interesting part.

## Open

Does exclusion survive non-linear demand, fixed costs, or free entry? None of the
three papers tests it. See [[open-questions]].
